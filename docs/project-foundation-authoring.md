# Authoring the project-foundation collection

Use this convention when maintaining project-foundation skills and their shared
routes. It governs source authoring; installed consumers should reach the needed
behavior in their skill without reading this document. Apply it to the affected
workflow as skills change. It does not require restructuring every member at once.

## Select the task before the subject details

An entrypoint must distinguish tasks that need materially different context,
outputs or authority. For each supported task, make clear what to read, what result
satisfies the request and when further work would expand its scope. Put concise
reading or application guidance directly in the entrypoint when that suffices.
Move substantial conditional workflows to references.

Choose the distinctions the skill actually needs. Contracts separates reading,
application, design and review. Diagrams also separates form selection, semantic
review and rendered readability. Bootstrap can remain a focused establishment
workflow. Do not create unused modes or require identical headings across skills.
Task selection must preserve requirements for the selected work: reducing reads
is not a reason to hide a necessary check or authority rule.

Reading answers from the existing artifact and its relevant sources. Application
assesses a particular case. Design establishes or changes decisions within scope.
Review produces findings for its stated question. A request can combine these;
follow the authorized work without silently turning a reading question into a
redesign or treating review findings as adopted changes.

## Discover and place gaps

Design and adequacy review must discover necessary decisions beyond the examples
listed in the skill. Trace a concrete use from the relevant participants' or
readers' perspectives. Ask where someone would have to guess, or where the stated
decisions could permit incompatible outcomes. Use domain-specific tests in each
skill; a completed template or checklist alone does not prove coverage.

Handle missing information according to the task. Reading states what cannot be
determined; application identifies the unsupported conclusion; design proposes
or establishes terms within authority; review demonstrates a consequential gap.
Keep consequential unknowns linked to their owner and dependent work. Continue
unaffected authorized work rather than turning each unknown into a global blocker.

Distinguish three cases:

- A **project gap** is a missing fact, decision or definition. Its home is the
  project's authoritative artifact or work queue.
- An **evidence gap** is missing support for a behavior or compliance claim. Its
  home is the verification work and evidence record; it does not amend obligations.
- A **guidance gap** is a consequential omission, misleading instruction or costly
  route in the skill itself. Use the collection's
  [friction capture and repair](../collections/project-foundation/references/friction.md)
  to record it and repair a demonstrated defect within existing authorization.
  Use pattern-capture when further assessment of a reusable lesson is needed.

Keep capture conditional on an observed problem. Ordinary missing project terms
and successful work do not require a skill-maintenance report. Do not claim that
an omitted topic heading proves the instructions inadequate.

Keep the discovery trigger reachable during ordinary consumption, including
direct skill entry. Entry hooks route to the shared friction owner; keep the
repair procedure there. Project instructions own standing source-maintenance
delegation. Source fixes and consumer pin adoption remain separate work.

## Grow families without duplicating shared rules

Select the task first, then load the relevant family or subject guidance.
A directory such as `references/er/` can own a diagram family's interpretation,
construction and examples. Add files only when there is enough distinct guidance
to justify them; a family need not reproduce every task as a file. Keep shared
layout, verification and renderer rules in one home, loaded when needed.

The same arrangement can serve contract families with distinct exchange semantics.
Select them by the exchange's actual properties. Retry identity, concurrency and
compatibility can span file, human and service handoffs; keep such cross-cutting
obligations shared instead of repeating them under every family. Load multiple
relevant references when an exchange combines properties. Do not force mutually
exclusive types or create empty directories for anticipated future topics.

A new discoverable skill needs a distinct user task and a useful independent
entrypoint. Split when routing trials show persistent confusion or unnecessary
loading that internal references cannot adequately resolve, or when independent
adoption becomes a real requirement. More reference directories alone do not
justify a split. Keep selection guidance authoritative when comparing forms or
families; a split must preserve that route and avoid competing definitions.

## Validate the boundaries

Use the repository's [routing trial method](../validation/discovery.md#routing-trial-method).
For a meaningful task-route change, exercise a narrow reading/application request,
a deeper design/review case and a nearby request that should avoid the broader
workflow. For gap discovery, use a consequential case not supplied as the skill's
worked example. Where family routing changes, verify both the needed deeper route
and a task that should avoid it.

Inspect actual outputs, missed obligations and unnecessary reads. Record limits;
one successful case establishes a route, not reliability or reduced context cost.
A prompted report of missing guidance does not establish spontaneous capture.
Preserve existing commitments through moves and compare affected instructions
with their prior version. Use focused fixtures, not a mandatory full cross-product
of every task and subject.
