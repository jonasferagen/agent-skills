# Review a contract

Establish the review's question and scope: clarity, missing obligations,
compatibility or whether terms match participants' actual expectations. Read the
applicable agreement and relevant consumer evidence. A review produces findings;
apply changes when the user's request includes them.

For coverage, trace the affected exchange from each participant's perspective.
At each decision or recovery step, check that the terms provide enough information
to act. Look for a plausible case where participants could obey the agreement
and disagree about the valid outcome. Use the obligation discovery in
[contract design](design.md#discover-the-obligations) when a broader completeness
review needs it; a focused review need not reconstruct the whole agreement.

For compatibility, compare actual old and new consumer expectations and retained
records. Use [failure and change guidance](design.md#preserve-identity-through-failure-and-change)
when revisions, repetition or interrupted operations affect the review. Added
fields or a passing schema check alone do not establish compatibility.

Distinguish missing or ambiguous terms from implementation that violates clear
terms and from unavailable evidence. For each material finding, name the relevant
term or omission, a concrete case, its consequence and the owner or decision
needed. Keep proposed corrections distinct from the current agreement.

Report to the user the cases examined and the limits of the evidence. A document
walkthrough can expose an inconsistent agreement; it cannot prove runtime
compliance. When the project has not decided a term, record the gap in the
findings and ask the user whether to decide it now.
