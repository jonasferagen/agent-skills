# Project policy

Use the project's authoritative principles, policy and instructions to resolve current
operating facts, adopted constraints, scope and exceptions. Reuse adequate owners
and formats. The project decides its policy; this skill supplies the workflow for
understanding, establishing and changing it.

## Select the task

| Task | Route |
| --- | --- |
| Read or interpret a rule | Use the applicable owner, scope and exceptions; distinguish explicit requirements from interpretation and unknowns |
| Assess compliance | Compare the relevant behavior and evidence with the adopted requirement; report met, contradicted or unverified claims |
| Establish policy or review its adequacy | Read [establishment](references/establish.md) to find consequential facts, decisions and derived requirements |
| Assess or implement changed circumstances or obligations | Read [changes](references/changes.md), including changes already observed in the implementation |

Infer the route from the task and load only what it needs. A narrow interpretation
needs no policy reconstruction or format migration. A literal correction needs
only a focused meaning check. When scope or operating circumstances change, use
the change route even if nobody has edited the policy yet. An adopted or
implemented design can expose such a change; a proposal remains a proposal.

## Resolve authority and evidence

Read the project's declared governing precedence. Where principles are its
highest commitments, derive policy from them rather than treating principles as
advice only when policy leaves room. A conflicting lower-level rule needs repair
at its owner; it does not silently amend a principle. Apply an explicit principle
to the decision within its scope, but do not infer destructive actions or removal
of unrelated protections from a slogan. Preserve unaffected commitments and
resolve ambiguous conflicts through existing decision authority.

A shared policy owner supplies common rules and the project's operating facts;
member repositories record only their local additions and authorized exceptions.
A closer file does not silently override shared commitments. Resolve conflicts through the project's established authority,
preserving user instructions and standing authorization. This skill adds no
approval gate and grants no permission to perform external actions.

Keep current operating facts, adopted requirements and observed implementation
distinct. A rule can be adopted but unmet. Missing evidence neither proves failure
nor establishes compliance; an implementation gap does not amend the rule. Use
the relevant implementation and verification guidance when behavior needs checking.

Policy owns the requirements selected under the project's principles. Operating facts stay with the policy owner,
or with the project's environment declaration when it keeps one; policy then
cites that declaration instead of restating its facts. Keep project-wide context
such as lifetime separate from facts specific to an environment. An access,
recovery or compatibility requirement is a policy decision, not an observed fact.
An explicit decision that a requirement is unnecessary needs its scope and review
condition; an undecided requirement stays open. Design and operating guides
own the implemented structure and procedures. Plans own pending work; work records
own dated assessments and evidence. Follow the project's document homes and link
these owners. Preserve prior decisions and assessed revisions when moving content.
Do not turn current-state documentation into a change history or a proposal.

## Handle gaps and finish

Identify whether a gap is a missing project decision, conflicting guidance or
missing implementation evidence. Name its consequence, owner and dependent work.
Ask only when the task depends on the answer; continue unaffected work. Do not
invent a guarantee, exception or operational fact to complete a policy profile.

For authored prose, follow the project's writing guidance. If definitions or
copies disagree, use its reconciliation guidance; clear-writing and source-of-truth
are suitable companion skills when available. Neither is required for a focused
answer from known policy.

Report the conclusion, relevant sources, checks actually performed and remaining
uncertainty. Retain an assessment only when the task needs a durable record.
Review alone does not commission remediation, and a policy document cannot prove
operational readiness.
