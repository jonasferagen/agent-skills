# Review: timeout followed by the same request

The agreement does not establish whether retrying a timed-out transfer is safe. Both teams could follow its identity and acceptance rules and still disagree about whether the retry represents the original delivery or another delivery.

The current [Delivery terms](docs/agreement.md#delivery) require a request ID, immutable revision and receiving team, with identity checked before acceptance. They expressly limit successful-copy evidence to transfer. The [Acceptance terms](docs/agreement.md#acceptance) give Mira acceptance authority for the requested revision and an identity-bearing receipt. A different revision stays pending; the sending team cannot accept it.

## Consequential missing terms

1. **Timeout outcome and reconciliation.** The agreement does not say how either team establishes the original attempt's outcome after a timeout. The copy could still be running, could have completed without a response, or could already have been accepted. The sending team cannot rely on a timeout as failure, cancellation or permission to retry safely. The receiving team cannot rely on the timeout as withdrawal of the request. The teams need to identify the authoritative status and evidence, who obtains it, and how an unknown outcome is reconciled before another attempt.

2. **Request identity versus attempt identity and duplicate handling.** Naming a request ID does not define its uniqueness scope or what a repeated ID, revision and receiving team means. A completed original copy followed by an identical retry could produce two deliveries. The sending team cannot rely on one effect or reuse of the original result. The receiving team cannot rely on the second arrival being a new authorized delivery, or assume it must discard it. The teams need to decide whether repetition returns an existing result, creates a distinct attempt or is refused, how attempts relate to the request, and how conflicting content under the same request ID is handled. The existing different-revision acceptance restriction still applies.

3. **Overlapping attempts and preserved acceptance.** A retry could arrive while the first attempt is still running; the first attempt's receipt could then arrive after the retry's receipt. No term coordinates those attempts or says how an earlier acceptance is preserved and reported. The sending team cannot rely on receipt arrival order to establish the request's current state. Mira and the receiving team cannot rely on a defined rule for consolidating duplicate copies and receipts into one accepted result. The teams need a rule for concurrent attempts, delayed evidence and replay of an already accepted result, with Mira retaining the agreement's acceptance authority.

4. **Recoverable evidence and its lifetime.** The agreement requires an identity-bearing receipt but does not require it to record Mira's acceptance, define a status lookup, or say how long request, attempt and acceptance records remain available. If Mira accepts and the response is lost, a later retry may lack evidence that distinguishes an accepted delivery from a copy awaiting acceptance. The sending team cannot rely on a copy receipt alone as acceptance or on obtaining the earlier acceptance later. The receiving team cannot rely on retained evidence being sufficient to identify an old retry. The teams need to specify the evidence of acceptance, its custodian and retrieval route, and retention consistent with the allowed retry period.

These are findings for the project owner and the two teams to resolve, not adopted additions to the agreement. The document walkthrough covers timeouts before completion, after copy but before acceptance, and after acceptance; it also covers identical, overlapping and delayed retries, plus a conflicting revision under the same request ID. No implementation, provider guarantees or runtime behavior was supplied or tested.
