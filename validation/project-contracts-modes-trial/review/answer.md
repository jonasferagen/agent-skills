# Review of publication handoff, adopted revision 1

The agreement permits multiple accepted packages but does not determine which package the distributor should expose as current. The planned use therefore permits incompatible outcomes while all stated obligations are met.

## Consequential gaps

1. **Authority to select the current package is missing.** “Editorial accepts it” establishes content acceptance, and “Publishing retains accepted packages” establishes retention. Neither assigns authority to select the distributor’s single current package or resolves disagreement between editorial desks. Desk A can accept revision 10 while desk B accepts revision 11, each against its own requested input. Either desk could expect its package to become current. Editorial and the distributor need an agreed decision owner and selection rule before either desk can rely on acceptance as authorization to publish.

2. **Out-of-order completion has no supersession rule.** Request identifiers and input revisions identify candidates; they do not rank competing requests or determine eligibility to replace the current package. Suppose request A for revision 10 starts first, request B for revision 11 is accepted and becomes current, and A finishes last. Making A current or keeping B current both remain consistent with revision 1. This could cause an unintended regression, but the agreement does not establish that a higher revision or later request must win. Editorial must decide the ordering or explicit selection policy; Publishing and the distributor must agree how it governs replacement, including overlapping decisions. Reliable concurrent handling depends on this decision.

3. **Distribution adoption and its observable outcome are undefined.** “Publishing reports completion after Editorial acceptance” does not require distributor adoption, name who performs it, or say how a requester learns that an accepted package was not selected. If B remains current after A is accepted, A can receive completion even though its package is never exposed. If adoption fails or its outcome is unknown, retention does not establish whether to retry or which package is current. Publishing and the distributor need to define adoption responsibility and authoritative evidence of the current package; the parties must agree what completion communicates. Downstream consumption and recovery cannot infer these facts from the existing report.

## Cases and limits

Examined: two independently accepted revisions, slow older completion, acceptance without adoption, and unsuccessful or uncertain adoption. The rejection rule protects accepted packages from rejected candidates; it does not resolve competition between accepted candidates. Repetition returns the existing candidate but does not establish which candidate is current.

These are missing obligations, not demonstrated implementation violations. Only the agreement and planned use were supplied; no runtime behavior was assessed. Selection by revision, request order, or explicit editorial decision remains unresolved. The adopted agreement is unchanged.
