# Seed model

Revision: 1 · 2026-10-03

## Meaning proposed for review

The reported 82 percent describes germination observed in a sample from lot A
under its test conditions. It does not establish a germination percentage for
all Dawn seed, for untested lot B, or a guaranteed outcome for a customer.
The former proposal, cultivar name plus one germination percentage, cannot
express those distinctions and is replaced by the following conceptual proposal.

A **cultivar** names the variety: Dawn. A **seed lot** identifies the particular
group of seed being sampled: A and B remain distinct lots even though both share
that cultivar. A **sample** identifies the seed selected from a particular lot.
A **germination observation** describes a test of that sample, including the
reported proportion and the circumstances needed to interpret it.

For this slice, each lot has one cultivar and a cultivar can have several lots.
Each sample comes from one lot; a lot can have no samples or several samples.
An observation refers to the sample tested. Repeating a test requires a distinct
observation; it does not change the identity of the cultivar or lot. Whether the
same physical sample can be tested again is outside the evidence available here.

## Observed case

Lots A and B share cultivar Dawn. One sample from A has a reported germination
result of 82 percent. B was not tested, so its germination result is unknown.
The shop draft “Dawn: 82 percent germination” loses the sample and lot scope.

The sample size, germinated count, sampling method, test date, conditions and
criterion for germination are not provided. Representativeness and applicability
to seed currently offered for sale are also unestablished. These gaps prevent a
supported lot-wide or cultivar-wide claim. A factual internal description is
“A sample from lot A of Dawn was reported at 82 percent germination.” This is
an explanation of the evidence, not approved customer copy.

## If another sample disagrees

Hypothetical case: a second sample from A is reported at 65 percent. Both
observations remain evidence about their respective samples; neither automatically
becomes the single truth for A or Dawn. Review sampling and test circumstances,
possible errors, and comparability before interpreting the difference. Averaging
the percentages or selecting the newest or highest result needs an explicit
justification and adequate underlying evidence; disagreement alone supplies none.
Any proposed shop claim relying on the first result must be reconsidered.

If the disagreeing sample instead comes from B, it adds evidence about B. It does
not invalidate the observation from A or merge the two lots into one cultivar
percentage. Missing sample provenance leaves even that attribution unresolved.

If review discovers an erroneous observation, identify that correction explicitly.
If both are valid, retain their distinct scope and the unresolved interpretation.
This requires conceptual distinctions, not a general history or storage system.
